# A bedtime story that teaches one coding idea

My first app made bedtime stories where robot siblings teach a kid one programming idea. You don't need the app to do it. Paste this into any chat model, fill in the three brackets, and read what comes back before you read it aloud.

```text
Write a bedtime story for a [age]-year-old. It takes about [5] minutes to read aloud.

The characters are two robot siblings who live in a small, friendly world. One of them is learning [pick one: if/else, loops, variables]. The other helps.

Teach the idea through what happens in the story, never as a lecture:
- if/else: a robot has to choose, and each choice leads somewhere different.
- loops: a robot repeats a step until something changes, and has to notice when to stop.
- variables: a robot keeps something in a labeled box, and what is in the box changes as the story goes.

Rules:
- Use the idea at least three times, a little differently each time.
- Keep the words simple enough for the age above. No code on the page.
- Nothing scary. End with both robots safe and sleepy.
- After the story, add one question I can ask before lights out that checks whether the idea landed.
```

## Try it

Run it once with each idea on three nights. Use the question at the end, and listen to how your kid explains it back. If the answer is right, the story worked. If it is close, ask for the same idea again with one change: "make the choice matter more" or "make the robot forget to stop".

## Keep it safe

Leave real names, your street and your kid's school out of the prompt. The robots can carry the story without them.
